02
Why this matters

Journey’s current setup cannot scale.

Excel, PDFs, WhatsApp, and personal phones are fine early on. They break when the portfolio grows.

Current state

  • Rent records spread across spreadsheets
  • Leases stored as scattered PDFs
  • Tenant messages trapped in WhatsApp / phones
  • Lease expirations managed reactively
  • No true system of record for rooms, tenants, and payments

Future state

  • One database for buildings, units, rooms, beds, tenants, leases, and rent
  • Tenant portal for rent, maintenance, leases, and documents
  • Automated rent, reminders, alerts, and renewal workflow
  • Centralized communication visible to the whole team
  • Property-level P&L for master lease margin

04
Decision logic

Choose based on Journey’s current size and pain point.

Are we at / near 50 units or beds?
Yes → AppFolio Plus / Max

Best long-term operating system if the minimum and pricing make sense.

Are we below AppFolio’s threshold?
Yes → Hemlane

Fastest clean upgrade from Excel, WhatsApp, and PDFs into one usable platform.

Is phone/chat chaos the biggest issue?
Yes → Rent Manager

Best fit when the team needs operational tracking, communication history, and exception handling.

Do we want a coliving-native experiment?
Yes → ColivHQ pilot

Test 1–2 properties only. Do not rely on it until U.S. payment, screening, and support are proven.

05
What the system must do

The non-negotiables.

Any vendor that cannot handle these should be removed from consideration.

01

Room / bed leasing

Separate tenant leases, ledgers, move-in dates, move-out dates, and payments inside one apartment.

02

Rent collection

Autopay, recurring rent, late fees, security deposits, and international payment backup.

03

Tenant portal

Pay rent, sign leases, submit maintenance, view documents, and communicate in one place.

04

Lease expiration alerts

30/60-day alerts that trigger renewal, replacement, listing, and turnover workflows.

05

Document storage

Central lease vault with e-signature, tenant IDs, applications, and compliance docs.

06

Central communication

Stop relying on personal phones. Every message and request must live in the company system.

06
Recommended system stack

Do not make one tool do everything.

Use one core PMS as the system of record. Add a separate CRM only for building-owner / master-lease deal sourcing.

1

Core PMS

AppFolio, Hemlane, or Rent Manager. This owns tenants, rooms, leases, rent, documents, maintenance, and renewals.

2

Owner CRM

Zoho, Pipedrive, or HubSpot for building owners, master lease prospects, follow-ups, and deal pipeline.

3

Payment backup

Native PMS payments first. Stripe, Flywire, or Wise as backup for foreign-card / international rent issues.

4

Website / portal

Use the PMS portal first. Build custom website pages around it once operations are stable.

5

Automation

n8n or Zapier after clean data exists: lease alerts, vacancy workflows, turnover tasks, and CRM sync.

Core principle: First create the system of record. Then automate. Automating messy data just creates prettier chaos.

07
Why not Buildium / DoorLoop?

They are good tools, but not for Journey’s exact model.

Buildium

Good general PMS. Weak fit for shared-room structure.

Buildium can work through a “bedroom = unit” workaround, but that can make accounting, pricing, and shared-apartment management messier.

Good for normal rentalsNot ideal for by-bed
DoorLoop

Modern and easy. But the leasing model is the issue.

DoorLoop is attractive for smaller landlords, but true overlapping separate leases inside one unit are the concern for Journey’s shared-room operation.

Simple UIRoommate limits

Boss version: Do not choose software because it looks clean. Choose software that understands one apartment can contain multiple unrelated tenants with separate leases and separate payments.

08
Demo test

The exact workflow every vendor must prove.

Do not let vendors sell from a feature list. Make them run Journey’s real workflow live.

“Show us building → apartment → room/bed → tenant → lease → payment → expiration alert → turnover → replacement tenant.”
1. Master lease setupCreate a building with guaranteed rent owed to the owner.
2. Shared apartmentCreate one apartment with multiple separately rented rooms/beds.
3. Tenant onboardingApplication, approval, lease generation, e-signature, and document storage.
4. Rent collectionRecurring rent, deposit, late fee, and international-card scenario.
5. Expiration alertShow 30/60-day alert and renewal or replacement workflow.
6. TurnoverMove-out, cleaning/renovation task, make-ready status, relisting.
09
Rollout plan

Implementation should be phased, not chaotic.

The goal is not to automate everything on day one. The goal is to get clean data into one system first.

Phase 1 · Setup

Build the database

  • Buildings
  • Apartments / units
  • Rooms / beds
  • Tenants and contacts
  • Current lease dates
Phase 2 · Money + documents

Turn on operations

  • Recurring tenant rent
  • Owner payables
  • Security deposits
  • Lease templates
  • Signed lease storage
Phase 3 · Automation

Scale the workflow

  • Tenant portal
  • Online payments
  • Lease expiration alerts
  • Renewal workflows
  • Turnover / make-ready tasks

Required data model: Building → apartment/unit → room/bed → resident → lease → recurring charges/payables.

10
Risks to verify

Four questions before signing.

Risk 1

Unit minimum

Does AppFolio count beds/rooms favorably enough for Journey to clear the 50-unit economics?

Risk 2

Foreign payments

Can international students pay rent without a U.S. bank account? What are the real card fees?

Risk 3

Room-level leases

Is it truly separate leases and ledgers per room/bed, or just a workaround?

Risk 4

Implementation cost

What are setup fees, training costs, required add-ons, and support tiers?

Decision rule: No vendor should be selected until they prove room-level leasing, international payment handling, and expiration/turnover workflow in a live demo.
